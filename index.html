Tribute to Dr. Rita Green

A prominent British expert, educator, and author in the field of Applied Linguistics and Language Testing, who was my professor teaching the subject of Data Statistical Analysis during the post-graduate diploma course at Lancaster University. She is an expert in the field of language testing and has trained test development teams in numerous projects around the world including those in the fields of education, diplomacy, air traffic control and the military.

Academic Contributions

Green’s work focuses on bridging academic measurement theory with everyday classroom practice. Green has introduced the PRICE acronym to define what makes a quality assessment system:

  • P: Practicality: Is the test realistic to design, administer, and score given the available resources, time, and budget?
  • R: Reliability: Does the test yield consistent, trustworthy results across different administrations and raters?
  • I: Impact / Beneficial Consequences: Does taking the test lead to positive educational outcomes and teaching practices (avoiding negative "washback")?
  • C: Construct Validity: Does the assessment accurately measure the specific language ability or skill it claims to measure?
  • E: Ethics / Equity: Is the test fair, unbiased, and transparent for all test takers regardless of background?

Publications and Professional Affiliations

Rita Green has published two books for language testers:

  1. Statistical Analyses for Language Testers, 2013, Palgrave Macmillan
  2. Listening Tests: A Practical Approach, 2017, Palgrave Macmillan (Springer Nature)

Rita Green has been a Visiting Teaching Fellow and Course Director of Language Testing at Lancaster University, UK and the past EALTA Expert Member.
